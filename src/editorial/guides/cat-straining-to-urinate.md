Repeated squatting, crying, licking around the genitals or passing only drops can signal a urinary obstruction. A blocked cat can become critically ill. Male cats are especially at risk, but any cat with uncertain urine output and repeated straining deserves urgent advice. Do not wait for a visible puddle of blood or for the cat to stop eating.

## Call and leave for assessment
Say “My cat is repeatedly trying to urinate and I have not seen a normal amount.” This is more useful than asking for an appointment for constipation. Mention sex, previous urinary problems and when you last saw this particular cat pass a normal stream. If you cannot distinguish urine from stool straining, say exactly that. The team can direct you to an appropriate service.

## Use the tray as a clue, not a test
One small wet patch does not exclude a partial or evolving blockage. Clumps from another cat in a shared tray do not confirm this cat can urinate. Do not press or squeeze the abdomen, give leftover antibiotics or attempt to flush the bladder by making the cat drink. Use a secure carrier and keep handling gentle. Bring prior records if already to hand.

## After the emergency visit
Ask the clinician to explain the working cause, how urine output will be assessed before discharge, and which signs mean immediate return. Repeated straining after discharge merits a call even if it looks similar to an earlier episode. Advice on water, diet and household stress depends on the diagnosis.

**Would a urine sample help?** The clinic can decide what collection method it needs. Never postpone evaluation to wait beside the litter tray. Your observation of repeated attempts is enough reason to seek help.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>What if it is the middle of the night?</summary>
<p>Phone the after-hours service and use the words “possible urinary obstruction.” If your regular clinic’s recorded message gives a referral number, use it immediately. Tell them how long travel will take and whether the cat has become weak or vomited. Do not arrange only a routine appointment for the following day.</p>
</details>
