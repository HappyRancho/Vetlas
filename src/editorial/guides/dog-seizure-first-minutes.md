When a dog falls and starts paddling, the event can feel much longer than it lasts. Start a timer immediately. If another adult is present, one person can call the clinic while the other clears the space. A precise duration helps the veterinary team decide how urgently treatment is needed.

## Protect the space around the dog
Move chairs away and block access to stairs or water if you can do so safely. Keep children and other pets out. Never put fingers, a spoon, medicine or water into the mouth. A dog does not need its tongue pulled forward and can bite involuntarily. Avoid pinning the body down. Take a short video only when the area is safe and another task does not need your hands.

## Know the emergency threshold
A seizure that reaches five minutes is an emergency. Repeated seizures, particularly without full recovery in between, also need emergency care. Contact the clinic early so someone can prepare transport. Follow a prescribed rescue plan only if it was written for this dog. A first seizure warrants veterinary assessment even if it stops quickly and the dog appears normal.

## Record recovery separately
Disorientation, pacing or temporary difficulty seeing can occur afterwards. Keep the area dim and quiet and avoid putting your face near the dog. Note when the movements stopped and when the dog could recognise you and walk normally. These are two different times.

| Detail for the clinician | Example of a useful observation |
| --- | --- |
| Immediately beforehand | Asleep, recently exercised, possible access to a medicine or toxin |
| During the episode | Time on the clock, limbs involved, response to voice |
| After the episode | Confused for a measured interval, another event, injuries |

Bring the original packaging if exposure is possible. A single video or normal interval between seizures cannot establish the cause.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Was it a seizure or a faint?</summary>
<p>Some fainting episodes also involve brief movements. Note whether the dog suddenly went limp or stiff, what activity preceded the fall and how quickly awareness returned. Avoid using these observations to rule out either cause yourself. They help the clinician decide whether to investigate, for example, the heart or the nervous system.</p>
</details>
