Start with a comfortable mouth. Bleeding, marked bad breath, dropping food, pawing at the face or pulling away when the muzzle is touched deserves a veterinary examination. Brushing a painful tooth can teach a dog to fear the brush and cannot remove disease below the gumline.

## Make handling predictable
Choose a quiet moment rather than approaching a sleeping dog. First reward a brief touch to the cheek. In later sessions lift the lip for a second and reward calm acceptance. Let the dog investigate a soft dog toothbrush and pet toothpaste. Human toothpaste is designed to be spat out and can contain unsuitable ingredients. Stop before the dog feels the need to escape or snap.

## Work along the outside surfaces
Begin with a few easily reached teeth. Gently brush where the tooth meets the gum, using small movements. You can reach many outer surfaces with the lips lifted and the jaws comfortably together. Build up to both sides and the back teeth over several sessions. Daily brushing is the goal because plaque repeatedly forms. A few successful seconds are a better starting point than wrestling through an entire mouth.

| If this happens | Adjust the next step |
| --- | --- |
| Dog tolerates lip lifting but hates the brush | End at lip lifting and introduce the brush separately. |
| One side is suddenly painful | Pause and arrange an oral examination. |
| Hard brown material stays in place | Ask about a dental assessment. Do not scrape it at home. |

## Will brushing replace a dental cleaning?
Brushing helps control soft plaque. It cannot assess a tooth root, measure pockets under the gum or treat an infected tooth. Ask the clinic to show the technique on your own dog and explain what professional examination or treatment is appropriate.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Which teeth should the vet show me?</summary>
<p>Ask to see the outer surfaces of the large upper cheek teeth and how to lift the lip without opening the jaw. A demonstration on a comfortable dog is easier to repeat than a diagram alone. If the clinician finds disease, get a plan for treatment and ask when brushing can safely begin afterwards.</p>
