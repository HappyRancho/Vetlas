A dog that pauses before a shiny floor may still walk happily on grass. That difference is useful. Watch one ordinary morning without encouraging the dog to perform. Where does it hesitate, slide, need two attempts to stand or abandon a favourite resting place? Start where difficulty happens most often.

## Change the route before adding equipment
Put a non-slip runner on the actual path between bed, door and water. Secure its edges. A loose rug that travels with the dog creates another hazard. Offer a resting surface the dog can step onto comfortably and a drink on the same level. Gate unsafe stairs and ask the vet or a rehabilitation professional whether a ramp is suitable. A steep, slippery ramp solves little.

## Measure comfort with a familiar task
Pick two daily activities, such as rising after a nap and walking to the gate. Describe how they look on a good day. Repeat observations at a similar time rather than testing how high the dog can jump. Short clips from the side can show the vet changes that are hard to reproduce on a clinic floor. Keep exercise consistent with the plan made for this individual dog.

## When does a bad day need a call?
A sudden inability to bear weight, marked distress, a fall followed by weakness, or rapid deterioration deserves prompt veterinary advice. Chronic arthritis does not explain every new limp. If prescribed pain control seems insufficient, report what changed and when the last medicine was given. Human anti-inflammatory tablets can cause serious harm in pets. Let the clinic adjust the plan.

At the next visit, ask which change would count as meaningful improvement, when to review weight and muscle, and what to do during a flare. That gives two caregivers the same practical goal.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Should I buy a harness or a new bed first?</summary>
<p>Look for the point where assistance would actually change the dog’s day. A raised bed with a high lip can make access worse for a dog that cannot lift its feet. A support harness needs to fit the dog and suit the person doing the lifting. Ask the vet to demonstrate safe support, especially after a fall or an operation.</p>
</details>
