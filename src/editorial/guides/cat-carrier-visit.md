For many cats, opening the cupboard that holds the carrier is enough to start a chase. A useful change happens long before appointment day. Leave a stable carrier open near a place the cat already rests. Put familiar bedding inside and allow the cat to enter and leave freely. A carrier with a removable top gives the clinical team more options at the other end.

## Practise in small pieces
First reward approaching the carrier. On another occasion, reward stepping inside. Work up to closing and reopening the door briefly, then lifting the carrier level and setting it down. Stop while the cat is still comfortable. Training sessions are easier when you have no appointment deadline. If food is the reward, check fasting instructions for the actual visit.

## Plan for the morning
Prepare the carrier before bringing the cat into a quiet room with the doors closed. Put an absorbent layer under the familiar bedding and pack a spare. Support the base with both hands. Secure the carrier during travel so it cannot slide or tip. At reception, keep distance from unfamiliar dogs and ask whether a quieter waiting spot is available. A light cover can offer privacy if ventilation remains clear.

## What if the first attempt fails?
Repeated pursuit makes the next attempt harder. For a routine appointment call the practice, describe what happened and ask about a handling plan or prescribed pre-visit medication for next time. Never borrow another cat’s sedative. For an emergency, tell the clinic both the symptom and the handling difficulty immediately. A cat repeatedly attempting to urinate without producing urine cannot wait for a training session. Tell the team if breathing changes during transport.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Would a removable top help at the clinic?</summary>
<p>Tell the practice if your cat settles in the carrier base. Some examinations can begin there with the top removed. Point out which clips open before anyone tries to tip the cat out. A secure, escape-resistant carrier is still essential for the journey; do not open it in the car park.</p>
</details>
