The best reference for tomorrow is how the wound looked when your dog came home. Before leaving the clinic, ask someone to show you its full length, point out any expected bruising or swelling, and explain whether there are visible stitches, buried stitches, staples or a drain. Those details change what you should look for.

## Make a useful first record
With your dog settled and the area visible, take one photograph in good light. Avoid pulling at skin edges or rolling a painful animal over for the camera. Each day, compare photographs taken from a similar angle and distance. Note how your dog moves, eats and rests alongside the appearance of the skin. A photograph cannot show tenderness or how unwell a dog feels.

| What you find | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Edges together, appearance much like discharge | Continue the clinic’s activity and protection plan. |
| Increasing redness, swelling, fluid, smell or discomfort | Contact the surgical team promptly and send the comparison if requested. |
| Wound opening, ongoing heavy bleeding or collapse | Seek emergency care immediately. |

## Protect the repair between checks
A few quiet days can make an active dog feel ready to jump before deeper tissue has healed. Ask for the exact restrictions on stairs, walks and furniture, and the date they can change. Keep the site dry unless the surgeon has given different instructions. Alcohol, peroxide, powders and ointments can irritate tissue or obscure changes. An Elizabethan collar only helps if the dog cannot reach the wound while wearing it.

## Can the collar come off overnight?
A sleeping household cannot supervise licking. Follow the surgeon’s protection schedule and have the fit checked if your dog can reach around it or cannot drink comfortably. At handover, give the next caregiver the last photo, medicine times and the number that answers after hours.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>What if there is a drain?</summary>
<p>A drain can have separate instructions for fluid, cleaning and removal. Ask the surgeon to mark its position on the discharge photo and describe what output is expected from this particular drain. A tube that changes position, stops draining as swelling increases or is chewed needs a call. Do not push it back into place.</p>
</details>
