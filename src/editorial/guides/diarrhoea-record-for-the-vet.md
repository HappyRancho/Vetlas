“Five trips outside” might mean five large watery stools, or repeated straining with only a spoonful each time. The distinction helps a veterinarian decide what to ask next. Start with the last normal bowel movement and note how the dog is acting between trips. A loose stool in a bright adult and repeated diarrhoea in a weak puppy require different responses.

## Decide how urgently to call
Seek prompt veterinary advice for repeated vomiting, black tarry stool, significant blood, abdominal pain, weakness or an inability to keep water down. Young puppies and dogs with existing illness can deteriorate quickly. Suspected toxin or foreign-object ingestion needs immediate advice. Do not delay departure to collect a sample.

## Write the timeline in the order it happened
Include changes of food, scavenging, new chews, medicines and contact with other ill animals. Record stool volume, consistency, blood or mucus and whether there was straining. Include appetite, drinking, urine and activity. One clear photograph can convey colour and consistency better than a long description. Keep household members from adding unprescribed anti-diarrhoeal medicines to the mix.

## If the clinic wants a sample
Ask how fresh it must be, how to store it and whether they need samples from more than one day. Use a clean, leakproof container and label the dog and collection time. Avoid scooping up soil or disinfectant. Wash hands afterwards and clean shared toileting areas carefully. In a multi-dog household, identify which dog produced it.

**Should the whole family change the dog’s diet immediately?** Call for feeding guidance appropriate to the dog’s age and health. Several simultaneous food changes make the history harder to interpret. Decide who will feed the next meal, who will record the next stool and who will call if symptoms worsen.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>How can a photo be more informative?</summary>
<p>Use ordinary light and include enough of the surroundings to show approximate volume. A close-up of one red spot hides whether the rest was formed, loose or entirely liquid. Label the picture with time and dog, then put the phone away and assess the animal: alertness, drinking and repeated vomiting often change the urgency more than appearance alone.</p>
</details>
