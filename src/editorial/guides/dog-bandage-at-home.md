A bandage can protect a wound or stabilise an injured limb, but swelling and moisture can turn a good fit into a painful one. Before leaving the clinic, ask which toes should be visible, where the top edge should sit, and when the dressing must be changed. Take a photograph of the fit while it is fresh.

## Look at both ends every day
Compare any exposed toes with the other foot. New puffiness, spreading toes, unusual coldness or a change in colour needs prompt advice. At the upper edge, look for rubbing, slipping and skin bulging over the dressing. Notice chewing, sudden reluctance to use the limb, smell, staining and dampness. Phone promptly about any of these changes. The next scheduled appointment may be too late.

## Keep outside water outside
Use the clinic’s suggested waterproof cover for brief toilet trips, then remove it indoors. A cover left on traps condensation. Check after walking on wet grass as well as after rain. A wet or soiled bandage generally needs professional attention, even if the outside seems to dry later. Do not use a hair dryer against the limb.

## What if the bandage slips at night?
Call the treating clinic or its out-of-hours service. Describe the amount of movement, the exposed toes and the dog’s comfort. Do not tighten the wrap with tape or push padding back into place. Improvised pressure can compromise the circulation, and cutting through a bandage at home can injure the skin. Follow direct instructions for transport.

Set a reminder for the next dressing change even if the dog seems comfortable. Ask who will change it, whether sedation might be needed, and what activity is permitted until then. The look of the outer fabric cannot tell you whether the tissue below has healed.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Can a normal-looking toe rule out a problem?</summary>
<p>No. A tail, trunk or limb dressing can hide irritation within the wrap even when the visible skin looks normal. New chewing, an unexpected smell or a wet patch warrants a call in its own right. Tell the clinic what the dressing was meant to protect, not just the colour of its outer layer.</p>
</details>
