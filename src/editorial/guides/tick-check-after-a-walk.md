A tick can be small enough to feel like a seed caught in the coat. After walks in scrub, long grass or areas used by other animals, run your hands slowly over the dog while it is settled. Look around the ears and eyelids, under the collar, between toes, in the armpits and around the tail. Check bedding and the people who went on the walk too.

## When you find something attached
Part the hair and check that the object is a tick. Nipples, skin tags and small lumps can be mistaken for one. If uncertain, or if it sits on an eyelid or inside the ear canal, have a veterinary professional remove it. For an accessible tick, use clean fine-tipped tweezers to grasp close to the skin and pull straight out with steady pressure. Avoid squeezing the swollen body.

Clean the bite area and your hands. Do not burn an attached tick, cover it in oil or twist it with your fingers. If removal is incomplete, avoid digging into the skin and ask the clinic for advice.

## Record the exposure
Write the date, location on the body and recent walking or travel area. A clear photo beside a size reference may help if the vet requests identification. Over subsequent days and weeks report new lethargy, reduced appetite, feverish behaviour, lameness or unusual bleeding. A tick bite does not establish a particular disease, and a dog with illness may never have had a visible tick.

## Close the gaps in prevention
Ask which product and schedule fit the dog’s weight, age, health and swimming or bathing habits. Show the exact packaging of any product already used. A household with cats needs species-specific advice because some dog treatments can be dangerous to cats. Checking after walks still matters when a preventive is in use.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>What if the same dog keeps collecting ticks?</summary>
<p>Map the last few walks and check the actual dates of preventive treatment. Look for overlooked places where the dog rests, travel outside the usual area or a gap between doses. Take that information to the clinic rather than applying a second product early. Some products share active ingredients even when the brands look different.</p>
</details>
