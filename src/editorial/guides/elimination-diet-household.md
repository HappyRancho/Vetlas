A diet trial answers a narrow question: do signs improve on a controlled diet and return with a planned challenge? A different brand of kibble at every shop visit does not answer it. Ask the vet which prescribed or carefully selected diet to feed, how long to feed it and how improvement will be measured.

## List everything that crosses the lips
Start with the regular meal, then add training treats, dental chews, pill wrappers, flavoured supplements, scraps and access to another animal’s bowl. Ask the vet about flavoured medicines before changing them. Do not stop a necessary medicine to keep the trial tidy. Decide what everyone, including visitors, can use as a reward. A jar containing pieces of the approved ration can solve several problems at once.

## Make the trial observable
Choose signs that were present before the change. For skin disease, record scratching during a familiar quiet period, ear discomfort, sleep interruption and any concurrent medicines. For digestive signs, use stool consistency and vomiting dates. Keep a short weekly summary. A diet may appear to work because a separate treatment was started or a flare settled.

| Event | What belongs in the record |
| --- | --- |
| Dog steals another pet’s meal | Date, amount if known and what it was. Tell the clinic. |
| Dog refuses the trial food | Call early for an alternative plan. |
| Signs improve | Ask when and how the planned challenge should be done. |

## Why go back to the old diet?
Under veterinary guidance, recurrence with re-exposure can help establish that food contributed to the problem. Skipping this step leaves more uncertainty and may commit the household to an expensive restriction indefinitely. The vet should decide whether a challenge is appropriate, particularly after a severe reaction.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Is switching from chicken to a shop-bought lamb food a diagnostic trial?</summary>
<p>The name on the front does not identify every protein or guarantee that the manufacturing process suits a diagnostic trial. Tell the vet what your dog has previously eaten, including chews. The prescribed choice and the later challenge are what give improvement a meaning beyond “the dog did better for a while.”</p>
