A sticker in a notebook identifies a product but may tell you little about when or to whom it was given. A payment receipt may show a date without naming the vaccine. When adopting an animal or changing clinics, collect the pieces without merging uncertain events into a single confirmed entry.

## Ask for the original clinical record
Contact each previous clinic or shelter with the animal’s name, approximate age and any identifying number. Ask for dates administered, product and components, batch details when available, and the next appointment or certificate. Photograph complete pages so handwritten dates stay attached to the correct label. Use separate files for each animal.

| Evidence in hand | How to present it |
| --- | --- |
| Signed certificate or clinic treatment record | Keep the original and share a legible copy. |
| Product sticker without a date | Record the product as a clue and ask the clinic to confirm administration. |
| Family member remembers a puppy injection | Mark it as recalled, with approximate time and clinic. |

## Build the next appointment around exposure
Tell the vet about outdoor access, contact with unfamiliar animals, boarding, relocation and travel. The right plan depends on species, age, local disease risk, prior doses, product and legal requirements. Puppy and kitten series account for maternal antibodies that may interfere with an early vaccine response. An isolated injection or a date written on a shop receipt does not necessarily establish a completed series.

## What if a bite has already happened?
Bring the animal’s record, but do not delay human wound care and urgent medical assessment while looking for paperwork. Human post-exposure decisions belong with a qualified clinician. For a pet exposed to a potentially rabid animal, urgently contact the veterinarian and relevant local authorities. At the end of a routine vaccination visit, ask for the actual administration record and a written next step before leaving.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Is a clinic reminder the same as proof of vaccination?</summary>
<p>A reminder says that a practice expected a dose to be due. It does not establish that the animal attended or that the product was administered. Keep the reminder as a lead to the original practice. For boarding or travel, also ask the destination which official document it accepts and how far in advance it is required.</p>
</details>
