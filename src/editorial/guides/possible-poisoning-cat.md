A chewed packet on the floor is a reason to call, even when the cat looks comfortable. Some toxins cause damage before obvious symptoms appear. Move the cat away from the product without exposing yourself. If a cat collapses, has a seizure or struggles to breathe, arrange emergency transport immediately.

## Tell the team what is known
Read the exact product name and active ingredients from the original container. A photograph of both sides is useful. Tell the vet whether the cat swallowed something, groomed a contaminated coat, walked through a spill or inhaled fumes. Give the cat’s approximate weight, the earliest and latest possible exposure time, and the largest plausible quantity missing. “Up to two tablets are missing, and there are two cats” is more honest and more useful than guessing one dose for each.

## Avoid adding a second injury
Do not make a cat vomit, give salt, hydrogen peroxide, oil, charcoal or milk, or try to neutralise a cleaner. Caustic chemicals can burn again on the way up. Ask about skin decontamination if the coat is contaminated and prevent grooming while following the clinic’s directions. Use gloves when appropriate. Keep the product sealed and separate from the animal in the car.

## Which household exposures are easily overlooked?
A cut lily bouquet, human pain tablets and dog-only parasite products are all relevant to mention. With lilies, tell the vet about contact with pollen or vase water as well as chewed leaves. For a parasite product, photograph the species and strength on the box. Brand names alone can cover very different preparations.

If the exposure occurred in a shared home, ask someone else to secure the substance and check other animals while you travel. A delay to prove ingestion can cost more than a call made on suspicion.

<summary>How do I report an uncertain amount?</summary>
<p>Start with what is physically possible. For example, a torn strip held ten tablets, six remain, and the last person to use it cannot remember how many they took. Report that range and who else had access. Do not discard the torn foil; the drug name and strength may still be printed on it.</p>
