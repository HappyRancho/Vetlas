A bird can still be sitting beside a food cup while eating very little. Empty seed husks may make the cup look full. Pet birds often hide early signs of illness, so a new combination of quietness, prolonged fluffing, altered droppings or changed perching deserves prompt advice from a bird-experienced practice.

## Compare with this bird’s routine
Notice where the bird normally sleeps, how it greets activity in the room and what its usual droppings look like. A dropping has a faecal portion, pale urates and liquid urine. Extra liquid is different from a poorly formed faecal portion. Food can change colour, but unexplained persistent changes or visible blood merit a call. A photo of a fresh cage liner helps more than trying to describe a colour from memory.

## Know when observation must end
Open-mouth breathing at rest, pronounced tail movement with each breath, inability to perch, collapse or bleeding needs urgent assessment. Keep handling brief, reduce noise and phone ahead to confirm the clinic can receive the species. Do not attempt a prolonged home examination, syringe-feed a breathless bird or use leftover antibiotics. Restraint itself can worsen respiratory distress.

## Prepare a useful handover
Tell the vet the species, usual diet and last time you saw actual swallowing. Mention new birds, household fumes, overheated cookware, cleaning products, cage changes and possible exposure to metal or chewed objects. Bring recent weights if obtained calmly on the same gram scale. Ask how to transport the bird safely and whether a fresh liner is useful.

**What about the other bird in the cage?** Tell the clinic about shared housing and exposures. Ask for individual advice on separation and monitoring. A healthy-looking companion does not rule out environmental exposure or illness in the first bird.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>Can I weigh my bird if it dislikes being caught?</summary>
<p>Ask the vet about training the bird to step onto a familiar perch or container placed on a gram scale while well. Record the empty container weight and weigh at a consistent time. For a bird already showing breathing difficulty or weakness, skip the weighing session and arrange urgent assessment.</p>
