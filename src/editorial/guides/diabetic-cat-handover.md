The most important piece of equipment in a two-person household may be one shared record. A prepared syringe, an assumed meal or a message saying “done” can leave the next person unsure what actually happened. Build the routine with the treating clinic while your cat is well.

## Match the supplies to the prescription
Have the clinic demonstrate storage, handling, measurement and injection using the exact product and device dispensed. Insulin strengths and syringe markings differ. Ask the veterinary team to watch you demonstrate the technique before you take over. Keep the written plan, contact numbers and a spare record together.

| Record immediately | Include |
| --- | --- |
| Meal | Time offered, amount eaten, vomiting or refusal |
| Injection | Actual time and prescribed dose given, or a clear note that it was withheld on advice |
| Monitoring | Glucose reading and units if prescribed, symptoms, action agreed with the clinic |

## Plan the awkward exceptions in advance
Ask for written instructions for a missed meal, vomiting, a missed dose and an injection that may have gone into the fur. Never repeat an uncertain injection or improvise a new dose. If the cat is not eating, call for instructions before the scheduled dose. If glucose monitoring is part of the plan, ask the team to show you which readings require immediate contact and how to confirm an unexpected result.

## Recognise a possible emergency
Weakness, staggering, disorientation, tremors, seizures or collapse can occur with low blood glucose. Follow the emergency plan and seek urgent veterinary help. Do not pour liquids or force food into a cat that cannot swallow safely. Bring the insulin, device and record so the receiving clinician can see exactly what was used.

A sudden reduction in insulin need can happen in some cats. Improvements in thirst or urination are worth reporting, but dose changes should follow the vet’s monitoring plan.

<details class="story-faq">
<summary>What if two different meters disagree?</summary>
<p>Write down each meter type, time and units and ask the diabetes team how it wants unexpected results confirmed. Do not average the numbers and use that result to change the dose. A cat’s clinical signs and a clinician’s emergency instructions take priority when a reading looks inconsistent with how the cat is behaving.</p>
</details>
