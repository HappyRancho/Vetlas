A cat scratches and develops small crusts along its back. The owner has carefully parted the fur and found nothing moving. That observation is genuine. It still does not exclude flea allergy. A sensitive cat can react strongly to saliva from very few bites, and an efficient groomer can remove fleas before anyone sees them.

## Why one animal may look worse
Cats sharing a home need not react to bites in the same way. One may be uncomfortable while a companion has little visible irritation. A different pet or an environmental exposure can keep the flea life cycle going. Indoor living reduces some exposures but does not seal a household off from them. The vet may look for flea dirt or other skin changes while also considering mites, infection, food-related disease and environmental allergy.

## What treating the household means
Ask for a plan for each species, age and weight in the home. Use only a product labelled or specifically prescribed for that animal. Some dog products, particularly certain permethrin formulations, can poison cats. Tell the team about previous products and dates before adding another. Ask about contact between treated dogs and cats and how to manage bedding and resting areas.

Adult fleas on an animal represent only part of the problem. Immature stages in the environment can sustain further exposure, so a single bath or finding no fleas the next day tells you little about long-term control. Agree when to reassess itching and skin damage.

## The useful follow-up photograph
Photograph the same area of skin in similar light and record whether scratching disturbs sleep or causes wounds. A pet that continues to injure itself needs further assessment. Persistent itching can mean ongoing exposure, another diagnosis or a secondary problem that needs its own treatment.

## When the bites are still appearing

Seeing a flea after starting control does not establish whether the product failed. The clinic will want the exact brand and active ingredient, dates of application, animal’s weight, whether the dose reached the skin as instructed, other pets in the home and any bathing around treatment. These details separate practical problems from continuing exposure or an unrelated skin condition. Take the packet to the recheck. Copying a neighbour’s treatment interval risks both under-treatment and unnecessary exposure.
