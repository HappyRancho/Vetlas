A 4 kg adult and a four-month-old puppy might weigh the same today. They have different nutritional needs. Choose the life stage first. For a growing dog, use expected **adult** size when the pack specifies a size class. This shortlist covers five different feeding situations using foods listed by their manufacturers in India.

## What earns a place on this list?

Each example has a public Indian product description with a specified life stage or format. The choices illustrate different uses. Comparative trials showing that one of these five produces better health outcomes for all dogs were unavailable for this comparison. WSAVA's nutrition guidance suggests asking who formulates the diet, how quality is checked and whether the manufacturer can supply calorie and nutrient information. A package photograph or an ingredient in the product name answers none of those questions on its own.

## Compare the cost of feeding one day

Suppose a 3 kg bag costs ₹900 and the daily amount for one dog is 150 g. There are 20 days in that bag, at ₹45 per day. These figures illustrate the calculation. Use your own price and ration. For mixed feeding, ask for the manufacturer's mixed-feeding guide or the kcal in each food. Adding a full wet ration to a full dry ration can double the intended energy intake.

## When a shortlist stops being enough

At your next weight check, bring a photo of the food label, the grams fed per day and a list of treats. This gives the vet something concrete to adjust if a puppy is growing too fast or an adult's waist is disappearing. A dog with a prescribed diet, unexplained weight loss or a sudden change in appetite needs individual advice before a food swap. If expected adult size is uncertain, as with many adopted puppies, ask the clinic to estimate a growth range and revisit it at the next weigh-in.
