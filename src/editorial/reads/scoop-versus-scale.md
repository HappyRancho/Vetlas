One person levels the scoop. Another leaves a small mound. A third adds a handful so that an old dog will take its medicine. Every person believes they are following the same plan. Small differences repeated over many meals can be substantial, especially in a small animal.

## An experiment with your own scoop
Weigh an empty bowl, zero the scale and add what you consider one normal scoop. Repeat with fresh scoops a few times and ask another caregiver to do the same. The point is to see the household’s variation, not to discover a universal correct number of grams. Equal weights of different foods can deliver different amounts of energy. Changing brands changes the calculation.

## Put extras in the same account
Start a day’s record at the first food offered and finish after the final bedtime treat. Include table scraps, training rewards, edible chews and medication foods. Ask relatives and dog walkers separately. For a shared meal, report that you cannot tell how much each animal consumed. A measured bowl and an unmeasured day are different things.

| Common observation | Better question |
| --- | --- |
| The bag says one cup | For which weight, life stage and activity level, and how heavy is our cup? |
| The dog is still asking for food | Is weight, body condition or muscle changing over time? |
| We feed half wet and half dry | Half by weight, volume or calories? |

## Use the follow-up to adjust
Ask the clinical team for an initial measured ration and a date to check progress. A body-condition assessment and muscle assessment add information that a scale alone cannot provide. A thin senior pet with muscle loss needs a different conversation from a healthy adult gaining fat. A new loss of appetite or unexplained weight change should prompt assessment, rather than another adjustment made only from the feeding chart.

## Make a repeatable handover

Put the agreed daily amount in a labelled container each morning and divide meals and training pieces from that allowance if the vet agrees. Keep foods for different animals separate. At the next weigh-in, report what was actually consumed rather than only what was prepared. If the plan changes, replace the note on the feeding cupboard instead of adding a second conflicting instruction. For a growing puppy or a medically fragile pet, confirm the plan with the clinic before reducing the ration.
