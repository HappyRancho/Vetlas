Imagine choosing between two diets. One bag has a photograph of fresh meat and a long list of recognisable ingredients. The other gives a clear life-stage statement and a manufacturer contact but looks unremarkable. Packaging makes it easy to compare stories and surprisingly difficult to compare nutritional suitability.

## Turn the bag around
First identify the species and life stage. A product intended as a complementary food or treat does a different job from a complete daily ration. Check the relevant nutritional adequacy information and feeding instructions on the actual pack sold locally. Similar names in different countries or product lines can identify different formulations. Save a photograph of the full label and lot details.

Ingredient order alone does not tell you the digestibility of the finished diet, its nutrient balance or whether its calorie density suits the animal. WSAVA’s nutrition resources encourage questions about the expertise behind a formulation, quality control and the manufacturer’s ability to supply information. Those are questions you can ask even when a marketing term sounds persuasive.

## Compare the ration actually eaten
A complete food can become an unbalanced overall diet when large portions are replaced by extras. Write down a normal day including biscuits, training rewards, chews and food used for medicines. For a mixed wet and dry diet, each package’s full daily recommendation assumes it is doing most or all of the feeding. Adding both full portions can overshoot the intended intake.

## What makes a good next step?
For a healthy adult, take the two labels and your dog’s recent weight trend to the next appointment. For a growing puppy, an ill cat or an animal on a therapeutic diet, discuss a change before buying a large bag. The most informative comparison is between the needs of the animal and the complete ration it will actually eat.

## A useful email to a manufacturer

Write down the exact product name and life stage before you call. Ask who formulates that diet, how the company checks finished batches, and how many calories are in a defined weight or can. Keep the reply with the photograph of the label. A customer-service answer about the brand’s philosophy is not the same information as an energy density or a description of quality control. If the food forms most of the diet, take unresolved questions to the veterinary team rather than guessing from the artwork.
