A good first question for a referral is “Who regularly handles this kind of operation, at which branch?” A surgeon who treats a fractured leg and one who operates on the bowel may work in different teams. These five examples have publicly described surgical work. The selection is based on documented scope and includes no national outcome ranking. The treating vet can help decide whether travel is safe.

## Send a referral another clinician can use

Ask your vet for the working diagnosis, the specific question for the surgeon, the actual imaging files and a list of medicines with the last time each was given. A photograph of a glowing X-ray screen can lose detail important for planning an operation. Send the original studies if available and ask the receiving team to acknowledge them. Keep a copy with you if you travel.

## Compare the whole episode of care

Request a written breakdown covering the procedure, anaesthesia, monitoring, imaging, consumables, overnight care and rechecks. Ask what can change the estimate once the surgeon examines the animal. For a fracture, clarify who will check the dressing and remove sutures after you go home. For a mass, ask whether laboratory examination is included and who will explain the result. These follow-up arrangements matter particularly when the hospital is in another city.

## If the animal is deteriorating

Difficulty breathing, collapse or uncontrolled bleeding warrants urgent help from a nearby veterinary facility. A long trip to a particular named surgeon can wait until a clinician has assessed transport safety. Scheduled consultations and emergency duty rotas can differ, so confirm the receiving team before departure.
