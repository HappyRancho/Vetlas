It is natural to ask which drug a practice uses. Yet two patients given similar medicines can have very different needs. Age, hydration, airway anatomy, existing disease and the procedure all influence the plan. The AAHA anaesthesia guidelines describe a continuum extending from the pre-anaesthetic assessment into recovery.

## Before the first drug
Give the team actual medicine names, doses and times, previous anaesthetic experiences and any recent cough, vomiting or appetite change. Ask for fasting and morning medicine instructions for this patient. A diabetic cat and a healthy adult dog may need different preparation. Pre-operative tests can identify concerns and change a plan, but normal results cannot make risk disappear.

## What monitoring is for
A monitor produces measurements. Someone trained to interpret them must notice trends, check that the readings make sense and respond. Ask who monitors the patient while the operator works and what the team follows, including breathing, oxygenation, circulation and temperature. A falling temperature or changing breathing pattern matters before it becomes an obviously abnormal number. Pain management is part of the same discussion.

## Waking up is an active phase
After the procedure, drugs may still affect breathing, temperature control and coordination. Recovery requires observation and reassessment. Ask where the animal will recover, which signs the team wants to see before discharge and who will call with an update. With a short-faced dog or a pet with respiratory disease, ask specifically about the airway plan.

A useful final question is “What would make you keep my pet longer?” It opens a discussion about the actual patient rather than a promised collection time. At home, follow the written instructions and contact the team promptly about difficulty breathing, inability to rouse the animal as expected or marked deterioration.

## The handover that matters at home

Before leaving, have the team distinguish expected drowsiness from a level of unresponsiveness that should prompt an immediate call. Ask when food, water and regular medication should resume, and which staff member can advise after closing. A recovering animal may be unsteady even if it briefly walks out of the clinic. Plan the trip and sleeping place accordingly. The useful discharge instruction is specific to that patient and procedure. A leaflet from a previous operation on a different pet may give quite different advice.
