A cat goes off its food, and someone says it has plenty of fat to live on. In cats, especially those carrying excess weight, the mobilisation of body fat during an abrupt shortfall in food intake can overwhelm the liver’s capacity to process it. Hepatic lipidosis is potentially life-threatening. The original reason for not eating also requires attention.

## The danger can hide behind the bowl
A cat sniffing dinner, licking gravy or arriving when the food cupboard opens may still consume very little. Measure what was actually eaten and note when normal intake last occurred. Recent stress, pain, a new diet, vomiting and underlying disease all matter. In a multi-cat home, a clean plate at the end of the day does not identify the diner.

Call the veterinarian promptly when a cat stops eating or takes markedly less than usual. Weakness, repeated vomiting or yellow colouring of the eyes, gums or ears increases the urgency. Do not try to force a large meal into a struggling cat. That can cause aspiration and deepen food aversion.

## Why vets sometimes discuss a feeding tube
Once the cause of inappetence is being investigated, reliable nutrition can be critical. A feeding tube may allow measured support over time without repeated restraint. It is a medical intervention with its own care instructions, not a signal that the animal can never eat voluntarily again. Ask how feeding amounts are advanced, how the tube is checked and which problems require an immediate call.

## A safer weight-loss plan
A stable cat that needs to lose weight should have a measured ration, nutritional assessment and scheduled weigh-ins. The target changes if the cat refuses the plan. Record body weight, appetite and the quantity eaten together. A falling scale reading alone cannot tell you that a diet is succeeding.

## A kitchen check that can prevent a misunderstanding

In a two-cat home, offer the prescribed meals separately for long enough to see who ate, provided both animals can do so calmly. Note whether the reluctant cat approaches, sniffs and turns away, tries to chew then drops food, or shows no interest at all. These do not diagnose the cause, but they give the vet more to work with than “the bowl was empty.” Save the previous and current diet labels when a change in appetite followed a new food.
