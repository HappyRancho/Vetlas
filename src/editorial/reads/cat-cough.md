A cat crouches low, extends its neck and makes a harsh repeated sound. Nothing comes up. Calling it a hairball is understandable, but it gives the clinician a conclusion before they have heard what happened. Coughing, retching and vomiting are different events and can look remarkably similar from across the room.

## Describe the movement before naming it
If the cat is stable, note what came before the episode, whether anything was produced, how long it lasted and what breathing looked like afterwards. A short video with sound can show posture and effort. Never trigger an episode to obtain footage. Open-mouth breathing, pronounced effort, blue or pale gums or inability to settle deserves emergency care. Recording can wait.

## Why asthma is part of the discussion
Cornell describes feline asthma as disease affecting the lower airways, in which inflammation and airway narrowing make breathing more difficult. Coughing and wheezing can occur. Other disorders can produce similar signs, so a video alone cannot establish asthma. A veterinarian may combine the history, examination, chest imaging and additional testing, choosing the approach according to how safely the cat can tolerate handling.

## The overlooked part of the history
Owners often focus on the loud episode and forget the quiet interval. Does the cat return to sleep comfortably? Is breathing faster than usual while genuinely asleep? Have smoke, aerosols, dusty litter or building work changed at home? These are clues, not proof of a trigger. Bring a list of exposures and current medicines. Do not try someone else’s inhaler.

For a cat with a confirmed diagnosis, ask for separate instructions for routine control and an acute episode. Knowing in advance when to call, when to use a prescribed rescue treatment and where to go is much easier than reconstructing the plan beside a distressed cat.

## What an inhaler demonstration should include

If the vet prescribes inhaled treatment, ask to see the assembled device, chamber and mask. Find out which medicine is for ongoing control and whether a separate rescue plan applies. Practise the handling sequence under guidance while the cat is comfortable. A struggling cat in breathing difficulty is an unsafe subject for a first training session. Write down what should improve between appointments and whether the number of coughing episodes, breathing effort or another sign is the best measure for this cat.
